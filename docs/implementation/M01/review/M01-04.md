## Findings

### F-14 — Cop blind spots — **DOWNGRADED to Medium** (was High)

- **Dimensão:** Correção / Segurança
- **Severidade:** Medium
- **Evidência:** `lib/rubocop/cop/opanel/unscoped_tenant_query.rb` now walks the
  receiver chain to its root constant (`root_constant`, lines 104-114) instead of
  requiring a bare const, and splits lookups into `BY_KEY`, `BY_CONDITIONS` and
  `TERMINATORS` (lines 49-64), so `where(id:)` counts only once something takes a row
  out of it. I re-ran my original 21 spellings and added 14 more, through
  `bundle exec rubocop -c .rubocop.yml --only Opanel/UnscopedTenantQuery`:

  **My original 21 leaky spellings: 21 of 21 now flagged** (was 6 of 21). Including
  the two I called idiomatic and blocking last round — `Team.kept.find(params[:id])`
  and `Team.where(id: params[:id]).first` — plus `unscoped.find`, `all.find_by(id:)`,
  `order/includes/joins/select/lock` chains, `find_by_id`, `find_or_initialize_by(id:)`
  and the string `"id"` key.

  The five legitimate forms stay clean, correctly: `TenantScope.for(...).find(id)`,
  `Team.find_by(slug:)`, `team.team_members.find_by(id:)`, `User.find_by(id:)`, and
  `Team.where(id: ids)` used as a relation. That last one is the right call — putting
  `where` in the lookup list would flag the boundary helper itself.

  Of the 14 new spellings, 9 are flagged (`first!`, `sole`, `where(id:).find_by(...)`,
  `limit(1).first`, `to_a.first`, `order(:name).first`, `find_or_create_by(id:)`,
  `where("id" => x).first`) and `exists?` is correctly not flagged. **Five leaky forms
  remain invisible:**

  | Written | Flagged |
  |---|---|
  | `Team.where("id = ?", params[:id]).first` (raw SQL condition) | no |
  | `Team.find_sole_by(id: params[:id])` | no |
  | `Team.where(id: params[:id]).pluck(:name)` / `.map(&:name)` | no |
  | `Team.where(id: params[:id])[0]` | no |
  | `ALIASED = Team; ALIASED.find(params[:id])` | no |

- **Why this is no longer blocking:** every spelling a developer writes while *trying
  to be correct* is now caught, which was my stated reason for the High. What remains
  needs raw SQL, a rare Rails 7 method, an array index, or constant aliasing — the
  last of which no practical static check catches. Holding High on a receding tail
  would be moving the goalposts, and would push toward a cop chasing a completeness it
  cannot have. The runtime helper (`TenantScope`) remains the primary control; the cop
  is the floor.
- **What is still worth fixing (the reason this is not closed):** `pluck`/`map`/`each`
  /`to_a`/`[]` all take rows out of a relation, which is the cop's own stated
  principle for `TERMINATORS` (lines 59-62) — so their absence is an inconsistency with
  the rule as written, not a deliberate boundary. `Team.where(id: params[:id]).pluck(:name, :slug)`
  returns another Team's data and is the most plausible of the five, because `pluck` is
  already how `app/queries/teams_for_user.rb:29` reads. And the docstring's "What it
  still allows, and why" (lines 28-34) lists four *legitimate* patterns and discloses
  no blind spot at all, so a reader concludes the cop is exhaustive. In a Story whose
  recurring defect has been "a verification that reads stronger than it is", an
  undisclosed tail in the checker's own comment is the same shape of problem.
- **Ação:** open — add the row-extracting terminators, and list the known gaps in the
  cop's docstring and in `spec/gates/unscoped_tenant_query_spec.rb` so the next author
  knows where the floor ends.
- **Estado:** open

### F-6 — `TeamPolicy` and `TenantScope` disagree about a soft-deleted Team

- **Dimensão:** Arquitetura / Segurança
- **Severidade:** Medium
- **Evidência:** `TenantScope#relation` (`app/queries/tenant_scope.rb:44-49`)
  delegates to `Team.accessible_to`, which starts from `kept` (`app/models/team.rb:42-46`)
  and therefore excludes `deleted_at IS NOT NULL`.
  `ApplicationPolicy#active_membership` (`app/policies/application_policy.rb:104-108`)
  and `TeamPolicy#team` (`app/policies/team_policy.rb:87-92`) apply no such filter:
  the Policy will happily allow `delete_team`, `deploy` or `manage_members` on a Team
  that the scoping helper considers gone. Nothing tests the combination.
  The Story's own framing — the report's table at lines 18-21 — is that the two
  controls answer different questions and are **both** mandatory; that only works if
  they agree on what a live tenant is.
- **Concrete damage:** unreachable today (nothing sets `deleted_at`, and the only
  Team-fetching path goes through `TenantScope` first). It becomes reachable the
  moment M11 adds `DeleteTeam` plus any Command that receives a `Team` from a job
  payload, an MCP tool or a background sweep rather than from the controller: the
  Policy will authorize a mutation on a deleted tenant, and the resulting write is
  the kind of thing a restore has to untangle. This is the foundation every later
  Story copies, so the divergence is worth closing now rather than per-caller.
- **Ação:** open
- **Estado:** open

### F-7 — The denial channel the product actually uses records nothing

- **Dimensão:** Observabilidade
- **Severidade:** Medium
- **Evidência:** `Opanel::Authorization.record`
  (`lib/opanel/authorization.rb:62-74`) writes `authorization.denied` with the
  classified reason — good, and tested at
  `spec/security/authorization_disclosure_spec.rb:109-137`. But the only place a real
  request is refused today is `TeamsController#show`
  (`app/controllers/teams_controller.rb:40-42`): `TenantScope` returns `nil`, the
  controller raises `RecordNotFound`, and **no authorization record is written at
  all** — no `actor_id`, no `resource_id`, no reason. The Story's Observability
  Requirements (line 52) ask for `actor_id, action, resource_type, resource_id,
  team_id` and a classified reason on a denial, and say it becomes an `AuditLog` with
  `result: DENIED` in M01-05.
- **Concrete damage:** an authenticated user walking Team external ids produces a
  stream of 404s and zero authorization signal. There is nothing to alert on, nothing
  to rate-limit on, and nothing for M01-05's audit trail to pick up — the enumeration
  attempt is indistinguishable from a user with a stale bookmark, in the logs as well
  as in the response. The classified-reason machinery exists; the path that needs it
  most does not use it.
- **Ação:** open — `TenantScope` (or the controller) should emit the same record with
  a `:out_of_scope`-class reason while still returning the identical 404 body.
- **Estado:** open

**Declaring the whole doc 04 §6.2 matrix.** Legitimate — data transcribed from an

## Verdict

**Critical = 0, High = 0. Nothing in this review blocks DONE or merge.**

Three rounds of fixes closed everything blocking. The cop went from 6 of my 21 leaky
spellings to 21 of 21, kept all five legitimate forms clean, and correctly refused to
flag `Team.where(id: ids)` as a relation — a distinction the second round got wrong and
this one gets right. The AC10 gate is deny-by-default classification with a reason per
rule. The generated-suite guard now asserts the truth about itself, which is the fix I
wanted and the one that mattered most: the recurring defect in this Story was
verifications that could not fail, and this round the response was to make one fail
honestly rather than to make the number look better.

Seven Medium findings stay open. None blocks, and none is invented: F-14's residual
five spellings, F-5's under-declared AF-07 list, F-6's soft-delete divergence between
the two boundaries, F-7's unlogged denial channel, F-8's dead `authorize!`, F-9's
inert `SCOPES`, F-10's untested `TeamMember` mapping. F-6, F-7 and F-9 are the three I
would carry into M01-05 rather than leave to drift, because each one is a foundation
behaviour that twenty Stories will copy before anybody revisits it.

I found no privilege escalation and no cross-Team leak in the domain code across all
three passes, and no test, assertion, threshold, lint rule or gate anywhere in the diff
was weakened at any point.
